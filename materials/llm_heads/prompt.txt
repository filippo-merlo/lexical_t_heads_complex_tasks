# What was measured

The model was given <<N_PROMPTS>> prompts of this task, made with the instructions above, that it answered correctly. At the last token of each prompt, just before the model writes its answer, we read the output of each head as a list of vocabulary tokens: the 20 tokens that this head's output pushes the model most towards. For each head, the table at the end gives:
- answer: the share of prompts on which that prompt's own correct answer (its first token) is among the head's 20 tokens;
- input: the share of prompts on which that prompt's input (its first token) is among the head's 20 tokens;
- tokens: the 20 tokens that are most often among the head's 20, each with the share of prompts on which it is there (1.00 = every prompt, .05 = one prompt in twenty).
Tokens are written as JSON strings; a token that begins with a space is shown with the space. Head ids are anonymous and the heads are in random order: neither says anything about where a head is in the model.

# How to score a head

Give every head a score from 0 to 100 for how clearly its tokens, across the prompts, represent the task.
- A head scores high when its tokens name the task, or the relation between the input and the answer, or the kind of answer without being answers (for example, a word meaning "city" when the answers are cities). A word that names the task counts even when the same word is in the instruction.
- The more of its tokens do this, and the higher their shares, the higher the score.
- A head scores 0 when none of its tokens has to do with the task: punctuation, formatting, numbers, function words, code, pieces of words, or unrelated words.
- Answers and inputs by themselves do not represent the task: a head whose tokens are only answers of this task, or only inputs of its items, scores low.

Do not lower the score of a head because it also shows answers or inputs. Mark it instead, in two lists:
- answer: the heads whose tokens include answers of this task (for any of its items);
- input: the heads whose tokens include inputs of its items.
Decide these marks by reading the head's tokens, not from its answer and input shares.

An example, for a task that is not in this study ("give the chemical symbol of an element"):
- " symbol", " element", " chemical", " abbreviation": high score, in neither list;
- " symbol", " element", " Fe", " Au": high score, in the answer list;
- only " Fe", " Au", " Na": low score, in the answer list;
- only " iron", " gold", " sodium": low score, in the input list.
